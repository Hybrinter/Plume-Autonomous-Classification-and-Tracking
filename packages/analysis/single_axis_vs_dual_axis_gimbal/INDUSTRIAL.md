# Industrial area vs latitude, and expected tracking time

TEMPORARY ANALYSIS. Generated by the industry command of
`uv run python -m analysis.studies.single_axis_vs_dual_axis_gimbal`. Not flight software.

## Physical setup

- **Fixed origin.** Elevation tracks the stack, or the cluster centroid of
  stacks. Wind can swing the visible plume around that point during
  the science window. Tracking plume CoG would add lateral walk this study
  does not model.
- **Covering disk.** Unknown wind azimuth makes a disk of radius **L**
  around each stack. Cluster covering radius is `R = D + L`, with D the
  haversine plant span. That is a possibility set, not a smoke-filled blob.
- **L = 2 km** is a **conservative visible-length envelope**
  (cooling-tower photo climatologies: typical 0.3-0.8 km, winter often
  >0.9 km, ~90-95th percentile ~1.5-3 km). Not a Mommert-chip measurement.
  Not typical length. Wind azimuth is unknown, so L is a radius: the
  plume CoG sits somewhere in that disk.
- **Any part of the disk.** Unknown wind puts the plume CoG somewhere
  in the covering disk. Acquire means: there exists a point of that
  disk the gimbal can put on the chip. One-axis: the parked FOV
  (+/-1.60 deg) overlaps the disk azimuth span. Two-axis: the ISS
  keep-out box (+/-10 deg) overlaps the disk, then the gimbal
  boresights that in-box point onto the chip. A disk that sticks out
  of the box still counts if any point is inside. This is not
  whole-disk-in-box and not whole-disk-in-chip.
- **P(visible)** = fraction of that disk in the FOV after pointing
  (unknown-wind geometry). Not computed as a yield weight here.
  Plume volume / Gaussian ribbon (~5% disk fill) is occupancy/SNR only.
  Do not multiply the two.
- **No locked design R.** Operational R is per cluster (`r_cover_km`) and,
  for lat tables, stack-weighted mean `R(|lat|) = D(|lat|) + L`.
- **Camera/lens.** BFS-U3-50S5, Sony IMX264, 3.45 um, 2448x2048,
  2/3-inch, global shutter; 150 mm athermal, 0.66% distortion. Catalog
  2/3-inch HFOV 3.36 deg is a check. 2x2 mosaic => band plane
  1224x1024, IFOV x2. Ignore lens-catalog 2.74 um.
  This is the purchased camera. SIL `ifov=0.02` is a stale placeholder.
- **Science stop.** Elevation window is one-sided
  90->45 deg (eta_max = 45 deg off-nadir).
  Extra caps: slant <= 650 km, along-track band GSD
  <= 50 m. Geometric Earth limb is ~69 deg off-nadir
  and is not a detection limit. Typical cooling-tower plumes are not
  segmentable at 60 deg off-nadir (~118 m along-track band GSD).
- **Tasking.** Opportunistic detect-in-chip hunt, not catalog cueing.
  Flight SCAN (az raster at el=0) is not the trade baseline.
- **Two slew caps.** Imaging rewind vs ground is the 1-pixel / 1 ms
  **band-plane** gate (current imaging gate). Hardware cap
  **10 deg/s** is not for science frames.
- **Reacquire.** On loss the gimbal immediately slews elevation toward
  45 deg (science limb) at the imaging rewind
  cap. Look-point ground speed is ISS motion plus ds/d(eta)*omega, so
  the FOV ribbon covers new ground faster than orbital motion and can
  acquire during the slew. After the stop, one-axis waits on ISS
  motion through the FOV ribbon; two-axis then rasters azimuth across
  the keep-out box (hypot of az slew and along-track scene rate <=
  imaging gate). Mean wait uses signed-lat stack density (peak near
  30-40 N). Cycle is dwell plus reacquire. Ocean-averaged Poisson
  spacing, not a city-corridor nearest neighbour.
- **Usable visit floor.** Contiguous TRACKING >= 1.0 s
  (~10 full-res cls+seg frames). The ~3 s stare number is no-gimbal
  FOV transit, not an inference floor.

## Inventory

Primary: Climate TRACE v5.10.0 CO2 **point sources** (2025, monthly summed)
that actually have smoke stacks:

- combustion electricity generation (coal / gas / oil / biomass / waste)
- cement, lime, glass
- iron and steel, aluminum, other metals
- chemicals, other chemicals, petrochemical steam cracking
- pulp and paper, other manufacturing
- oil and gas refining

Oil/gas production, mines, hydro/solar/wind, and food plants are out:
they are not the industrial-stack plume the payload is built for.

Nearby sources within 8 km are one plant cluster. Clusters wider than
the chip (~12 km covering radius) are split, matching the earlier rule
that two plants farther apart than the chip are two tracks. Covering
radius is `R = D + 2 km` (plant span + L envelope).

The size assumption is that industrial-area size scales with the
number of stacks, so the **primary weight is stack count**. Covering-disk
area over-weights sprawling clusters (pi D^2) and is a sensitivity only.
Emissions weight how much the plant is actually running.

### Single-target dwell and reacquire at R(|lat|)

T1 / T2 are how long **one** cluster stays in the science window,
using each band's stack-weighted mean R. After that target leaves,
the gimbal rewinds toward the limb stop and can acquire along the
path; two-axis then rasters azimuth. 1-axis limb search is the FOV
ribbon; 2-axis is the keep-out box at the smear-limited scan rate.
Lost time is T_reacq from **stack count in that signed-latitude band**.
Cycle time is dwell plus reacquire.
Same-origin leftover T2-T1 is not the lost-time metric.

T_reacq columns below are the **northern** band at +|lat|. The
southern band at -|lat| has far fewer stacks and a longer wait.
The signed-latitude figure is the decision plot.

## Expected times (ISS-belt clusters)

For each cluster, single-target dwell uses that cluster's own
`r_cover_km` and the same one-sided science window as the
geometry command. Reacquire uses **signed-latitude stack density**
at that cluster's lat, so 30-40 N (industrial belt) reacquires
faster than the equator or the southern hemisphere at the same
|lat|. A pass is assumed over the cluster origin for dwell;
off-track origins are a separate loss in the swath column.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **stack count (primary)** | 62.9 | 64.2 | 60.9 | 27.0 | 123.8 | 91.2 | 72% | 78% |
| 2025 CO2 | 63.1 | 64.1 | 45.5 | 22.1 | 108.6 | 86.2 | 75% | 80% |
| covering-disk area (sprawl) | 64.2 | 64.2 | 38.9 | 19.6 | 103.1 | 83.9 | 77% | 81% |

ISS dwell (more time near +/-i) does not change single-target dwell:
stacks + dwell -> **63.5 vs 64.4 s** (same-origin leftover 0.9 s).
Lost time is reacquire: **61 s (1-axis) vs 27 s (2-axis)**. Cycle (start of track to next acquire) is **124 s vs 91 s**. Duty T_dwell / cycle is **72% vs 78%**.
Ocean-averaged longitude in each signed lat band still makes empty
ocean stretches slow; city corridors would reacquire faster. This
is not a nearest-neighbour plant-to-plant model. Folding |lat|
would have made 35 S look like 35 N; it does not.

## Lateral swath (why 2-axis still buys something at 50 deg)

Tracking time is conditional on the cluster being in the frame.
1-axis only sees +/-1.60 deg (~12 km at nadir). 2-axis sees the +/-10 deg
box (~76 km). Ground-track spacing is thousands of km, so daily
longitude coverage is proportional to swath.

| | 1-axis | 2-axis | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mean daily coverage of a random ISS-belt cluster | 2.26% | 14.22% | 6.31x |
| primary FoM E[T_usable x coverage] (T>=1s) | 1.42 | 9.14 | 6.41x |
| geometry check E[T x coverage] (s x frac) | 1.42 | 9.14 | 6.41x |
| distinct usable visits (frac/day) | 0.0226 | 0.1422 | 6.31x |

The ~6x coverage ratio is the extra search area of the +/-10 deg ISS
keep-out box versus the 3.2 deg chip. Two-axis still has to scan that
box after the limb stop; scan time is in T_reacq, not in coverage.
The along-track science window is no longer 124 s (that was 60 deg
off-nadir). Combined FoM is dwell x coverage with the 1 s usable floor.

## Decision numbers

1. **Single-target dwell:** stack-weighted **63 s vs 64 s**.
   Almost all of the same-origin gap is the 20-40 N band, where Earth
   rotation walks the covering disk out of the 1-axis chip. That
   number is still the right 'how long can we stare at one plant'
   metric. Only ~10% of stacks sit at |lat| >= 45 deg.
2. **Lost time is reacquire, both gimbals:** after the target leaves,
   rewind toward the limb stop (searching the FOV ribbon), then wait
   on ISS motion (1-axis) or raster the keep-out box (2-axis).
   T_reacq is that two-phase wait at signed-latitude stack density
   (shortest in the 30-40 N belt). Stack-weighted
   E[T_reacq] is **61 s (1-axis ribbon) vs 27 s (2-axis box)**.
   Cycle start-of-track to next-acquire is
   **124 s vs 91 s** (duty 72% vs 78%).
3. **Primary FoM: inferred-usable plume-seconds/day.** Stack-weighted
   E[T_usable x coverage] with T_usable = dwell if dwell >= 1 s else 0. Ratio **6.4x** in favour of two-axis. The raw E[T x coverage] geometry check is
   **6.4x**. The ~6x in coverage is the keep-out box versus the chip, by design.
4. A polar-slice one-axis payload that is only tasked at |lat| >= 45 deg
   keeps nearly the full science-window dwell, but it is looking at
   ~10% of the world's stack-bearing industry. If the mission must
   work the 20-40 N belt, 2-axis is required for Earth-rotation hold
   and for the wider search box.
