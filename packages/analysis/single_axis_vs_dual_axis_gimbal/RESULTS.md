# Single-axis gimbal tracking time

TEMPORARY ANALYSIS. Generated by the geometry command of
`uv run python -m analysis.studies.single_axis_vs_dual_axis_gimbal`. Not flight software.

## Physical setup

- **Fixed origin.** Elevation tracks the stack, or the cluster centroid of
  stacks. Wind can swing the visible plume around that point during
  the science window. Tracking plume CoG would add lateral walk this study
  does not model.
- **Covering disk.** Unknown wind azimuth makes a disk of radius **L**
  around each stack. Cluster covering radius is `R = D + L`, with D the
  haversine plant span. That is a possibility set, not a smoke-filled blob.
- **L = 2 km** is a **conservative visible-length envelope**
  (cooling-tower photo climatologies: typical 0.3-0.8 km, winter often
  >0.9 km, ~90-95th percentile ~1.5-3 km). Not a Mommert-chip measurement.
  Not typical length. Wind azimuth is unknown, so L is a radius: the
  plume CoG sits somewhere in that disk.
- **Any part of the disk.** Unknown wind puts the plume CoG somewhere
  in the covering disk. Acquire means: there exists a point of that
  disk the gimbal can put on the chip. One-axis: the parked FOV
  (+/-1.60 deg) overlaps the disk azimuth span. Two-axis: the ISS
  keep-out box (+/-10 deg) overlaps the disk, then the gimbal
  boresights that in-box point onto the chip. A disk that sticks out
  of the box still counts if any point is inside. This is not
  whole-disk-in-box and not whole-disk-in-chip.
- **P(visible)** = fraction of that disk in the FOV after pointing
  (unknown-wind geometry). Not computed as a yield weight here.
  Plume volume / Gaussian ribbon (~5% disk fill) is occupancy/SNR only.
  Do not multiply the two.
- **No locked design R.** Operational R is per cluster (`r_cover_km`) and,
  for lat tables, stack-weighted mean `R(|lat|) = D(|lat|) + L`.
- **Camera/lens.** BFS-U3-50S5, Sony IMX264, 3.45 um, 2448x2048,
  2/3-inch, global shutter; 150 mm athermal, 0.66% distortion. Catalog
  2/3-inch HFOV 3.36 deg is a check. 2x2 mosaic => band plane
  1224x1024, IFOV x2. Ignore lens-catalog 2.74 um.
  This is the purchased camera. SIL `ifov=0.02` is a stale placeholder.
- **Science stop.** Elevation window is one-sided
  90->45 deg (eta_max = 45 deg off-nadir).
  Extra caps: slant <= 650 km, along-track band GSD
  <= 50 m. Geometric Earth limb is ~69 deg off-nadir
  and is not a detection limit. Typical cooling-tower plumes are not
  segmentable at 60 deg off-nadir (~118 m along-track band GSD).
- **Tasking.** Opportunistic detect-in-chip hunt, not catalog cueing.
  Flight SCAN (az raster at el=0) is not the trade baseline.
- **Two slew caps.** Imaging rewind vs ground is the 1-pixel / 1 ms
  **band-plane** gate (current imaging gate). Hardware cap
  **10 deg/s** is not for science frames.
- **Reacquire.** On loss the gimbal immediately slews elevation toward
  45 deg (science limb) at the imaging rewind
  cap. Look-point ground speed is ISS motion plus ds/d(eta)*omega, so
  the FOV ribbon covers new ground faster than orbital motion and can
  acquire during the slew. After the stop, one-axis waits on ISS
  motion through the FOV ribbon; two-axis then rasters azimuth across
  the keep-out box (hypot of az slew and along-track scene rate <=
  imaging gate). Mean wait uses signed-lat stack density (peak near
  30-40 N). Cycle is dwell plus reacquire. Ocean-averaged Poisson
  spacing, not a city-corridor nearest neighbour.
- **Usable visit floor.** Contiguous TRACKING >= 1.0 s
  (~10 full-res cls+seg frames). The ~3 s stare number is no-gimbal
  FOV transit, not an inference floor.

Also locked for this run:

- Elevation window is **one-sided** 90->45 deg (eta_max 45 deg).
  Hard gimbal stops for clearance; tracking time stops at the science
  limit (slant / GSD), with no leftover FOV walk-out past the stop.
- Mount is geocentric nadir. A <=5 deg offset is a later correction, not in
  this run.
- If plants are spread farther than the chip (~+/-12 km at nadir), they are
  two clusters. Do not average across a city.
- Current ISS TLE (Celestrak GP, epoch 2026-09-01). Earth rotation and
  51.63 deg inclination are on. Design pass is **max latitude** (heading
  due east), which is the polar-most nadir ISS can reach.
- Full-frame inference = the full 2x2 band plane. That does not change
  optical FOV; it is a compute note.
- A ~2028 launch still sits in the current 416-423 km operational band.
  ISS is committed through 2030; retrograde deorbit lowering is a
  2028-2030 lead-up, not a 2028 operations baseline.

## Imaging gate and slew caps (current imaging gate)

Science frames use a 1 ms exposure and a 1 **band-plane** pixel smear
budget. Scene-relative rate on rewind is gimbal slew plus ISS track,
so the imaging rewind cap is the smear limit minus peak elevation rate.
The hardware cap of 10 deg/s is a motor limit, not
a science-frame limit. This gate is the current imaging assumption;
a shorter exposure or a looser smear budget would raise it.

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Exposure | 1.0 ms |
| Smear budget | 1 band-plane pixel |
| omega_rel_max = IFOV_band / exposure | **2.636 deg/s** |
| Peak ISS elevation rate (design pass) | 0.911 deg/s |
| Imaging rewind omega_img | **1.725 deg/s** |
| Hardware slew cap | 10.0 deg/s |

## Hunt cycle (design pass, full-window loss)

On track loss the gimbal slews elevation toward the science limb at
**1.725 deg/s**. Look-point ground speed is ISS ground speed
plus ds/d(eta) times that rewind rate, so the FOV ribbon covers new
ground faster than orbital motion. After the limb stop, one-axis waits
on ISS motion; two-axis rasters +/-az_box at the leftover smear budget
(hypot of az slew and along-track scene rate <= omega_rel).

## Earth rotation vs pass latitude

At low latitude the ISS heading has a large north component, so Earth
rotation (always east) has a **cross-track** piece. That walks even a
nadir-centered origin in azimuth, peaking at the far look where range
is long. At max latitude the heading is due east and the walk vanishes.
That is why a polar-slice one-axis placement is the right one-axis case.

This table is a **point origin (R = 0)**. Operational covering radius
is `R(|lat|) = D(|lat|) + L` from the Climate TRACE plant span; that
profile and the T(lat, R(|lat|)) curves are in `INDUSTRIAL.md`.

For a point origin, Earth rotation walks it out of the chip below ~40 deg
latitude. Below that, one-axis only keeps the last tens of seconds near
nadir. How much a real cluster loses depends on R(|lat|), not on a
locked design radius.

## Covering-disk sensitivity (design pass)

Elevation tracks the fixed stack / cluster centroid. Acquire is an
existence test on unknown wind, not a filled-disk occupancy:

1. **Can we point?** One-axis: the parked chip FOV overlaps the disk
   azimuth span. Two-axis: the +/-10 deg ISS keep-out box overlaps
   the disk. A disk that sticks out of the box still counts if any
   point is inside.
2. **Is that point on the chip?** One-axis is already parked, so FOV
   overlap is the whole test. Two-axis then boresights the in-box
   point (az = 0 on the chip). We do not require the rest of the
   disk to fit in the box or on the chip.

Whole-disk-in-box was the previous 2-axis test and is wrong for this
gimbal: a large disk at the far look can span more than +/-10 deg
even when the origin is on boresight, so that test dropped dwell as
R grew. The chip-after-boresight test keeps full-window 2-axis dwell
whenever any point of the disk is inside the box.
The radii below are a **sensitivity sweep at the design latitude**,
not a locked operational R. Isolated stacks sit at R = L = 2 km.

## Full-frame inference

Optical FOV is the full chip either way. Full-frame inference changes
compute, not tracking time. The 2x2 band plane is
**1224 x 1024**.

| | 256x256 (today) | full band plane | scale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pixels | 65536 | 1253376 | 19.1x |
| DilateNet-w32 FLOPs | 0.21 G | 4.02 G | 19.1x |
| ShuffleNet FLOPs | 0.110 G | 2.10 G | 19.1x |
| CPU accept latency (linear scale from 256) | 2.3 / 3.4 ms | 44 / 65 ms | |

The 500x parameter cut (DilateNet-w32 23.5 k vs U-Net 13.4 M) is what
makes full-frame plausible. FLOPs still fit the 500 ms budget if latency
scales with pixels. Jetson Nano (4 GB) is tighter on **activation memory**
at 1224x1024 than Orin NX; that needs a bench, not a FLOP argument.
A 256-pixel centre crop on this optic is only ~0.67 deg -- that would
throw away the 1-axis lateral budget. Full-frame is the right inference
size for this lens.

## Bottom line

1. Along-track, one elevation axis gives **65 s**
   at the design (max-lat) pass, vs **3.0 s** staring.
   Stop at 45 deg elevation (45 deg off-nadir, slant 635 km). Peak track rate 0.91 deg/s
   is under the 10 deg/s hardware cap. Imaging
   rewind against the pass is capped at **1.72 deg/s**.
2. At that pass, Earth-rotation az walk of the origin is
   **0.003 deg**. Any-part overlap with the
   +/-1.60 deg chip keeps the origin in frame
   for the whole window -- **0 s lost** vs two-axis at this pass.
3. At equatorial and mid-latitude passes, Earth rotation walks the
   origin out at the far look. One-axis then keeps only the last
   tens of seconds, worse for larger R(|lat|). Use two-axis for those
   passes, or do not work them. See `INDUSTRIAL.md` for T vs R(|lat|).
4. If two plants are farther apart than ~24 km, they are two clusters.
   Track one. After it leaves the frame, rewind toward the limb stop
   and hunt along that path (see `INDUSTRIAL.md` for T_reacq).
5. Worldwide stack inventory (Climate TRACE 2025) is in
   `INDUSTRIAL.md`.
