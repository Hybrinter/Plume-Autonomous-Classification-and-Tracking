# Single-axis gimbal tracking time

TEMPORARY ANALYSIS. Generated by `analyze.py`. Not flight software.

## Questions that still change the number

These are the knobs that move the result by more than a few percent.
Defaults used for this run are in parentheses.

1. **Window sidedness.** Is elevation `[90, 30]` one side of nadir only
   (~one pass of ~100 s), or can a single elevation axis travel *through*
   nadir to 30 deg on the other side (about 2× the time)? (default: one-sided)
2. **Mount tilt.** Is the mount normal geocentric nadir, or is the payload
   already tilted toward the pole? A fixed off-nadir bias changes both
   dwell and GSD. (default: untilted nadir at el = 90)
3. **CoG disk radius R.** What radius should we design to? A single visible
   industrial plume is typically a few hundred metres to a couple of km;
   a plant cluster is a few km; a city-scale set of stacks can be 10–30 km.
   **This is the number that decides whether 1-axis loses any time.**
4. **What is the tracked target?** Combined centroid of every plume
   (`R_centroid = Σ w_i r_i`, so equal-radius plumes do *not* grow with N),
   the currently selected plume (then origin *separation* matters), or the
   bounding circle that keeps every plume in the frame (`R_cover = D + r`)?
5. **In-frame vs on-boresight.** Is a plume useful anywhere in the sensor
   FOV, or must the gimbal keep the CoG on boresight (the current TRACKING
   ROI crop assumes we can centre the target)?
6. **Altitude.** 400 km baseline, or the current ISS band ~410–420 km?
7. **After the elevation stop.** Count the extra seconds while the target
   walks out of the leftover along-track FOV, or stop at the gimbal limit?
8. **FOV source.** IMX264 active area (this run) or the lens-sheet 2/3"
   format HFOV of 3.36 deg (8.8 mm width, slightly larger than IMX264)?
9. **Full chip vs inference ROI.** Full 2448×2048 mosaic FOV, or the 256×256
   inference window the flight software uses today?
10. **Earth rotation / heading.** First-order spherical non-rotating Earth
    (this run), or a polar-latitude pass with Earth rotation and ISS
    inclination 51.6 deg?

## Assumptions used in this run

- Spherical Earth, radius 6371 km, no Earth rotation.
- Circular ISS orbit, altitude **400 km**.
- Mount normal = geocentric nadir at closest approach. Elevation window
  **90 → 30 deg** (60 deg off-nadir),
  **one_sided**.
- Azimuth operational box ±10 deg (two-axis only).
- Sensor long axis (2448 px) is **lateral** (cross-track). Short axis
  (2048 px) is along-track.
- Thin-lens FOV from IMX264 active area and 150.00 mm focal length.
  Distortion (0.66 %) is noted, not modelled.
- A ground-fixed stack origin. The tracked CoG stays in a disk of radius R
  around that origin. Worst-case CoG is the cross-track edge of the disk.
- One-axis: elevation tracks the origin; azimuth is parked at 0.
- Two-axis: both axes track the CoG while it stays in the operational box.

## Along-track tracking time (the elevation axis)

For a stack on the slice centerline the azimuth of the origin stays ~0.
The elevation axis is the one that buys tracking time.

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Elevation window | 90 → 30 deg (one_sided) |
| Time in elevation window | **107.5 s** |
| Extra time beyond the 30 deg stop (target still in the 2.70 deg FOV) | 8.1 s |
| Staring at nadir (no gimbal) | **2.65 s** |
| Peak elevation rate | 1.034 deg/s |
| Mission slew cap | 2.0 deg/s |
| Time from CA of window start / stop | -107.5 / -0.0 s |
| Slant range at start / stop | 894.4 / 400.0 km |

At 420 km altitude the one-sided elevation window is **114.2 s**
(stare 2.75 s). Altitude in the ISS band changes dwell by a few percent.

A two-sided elevation axis (through nadir to 30 deg aft) gives **215.1 s** of along-track track (2.00× the one-sided window), still with no azimuth motor.

Near nadir the target races through the 2.7 deg along-track FOV in a couple
of seconds. That is why the elevation axis is the one that matters for SWaP:
it turns ~2.7 s of staring time into **108 s** of track.

## Lateral loss of dropping the azimuth axis

The CoG of a ground-fixed disk subtends the largest azimuth at closest
approach, where range is shortest. If that peak azimuth stays inside the
sensor half-FOV, a one-axis gimbal parked at az = 0 never loses the CoG.

| R (km) | peak \|az\| (deg) | 1-axis time (s) | 2-axis time (s) | time lost (s) | lost fraction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 | 0.07 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 | 0.0% |
| 1.0 | 0.14 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 | 0.0% |
| 2.0 | 0.29 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 | 0.0% |
| 5.0 | 0.72 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 | 0.0% |
| 10.0 | 1.43 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 | 0.0% |
| 15.0 | 2.15 | 60.3 | 107.6 | 47.3 | 43.9% |
| 20.0 | 2.86 | 28.7 | 107.6 | 78.9 | 73.4% |
| 30.0 | 4.29 | 0.0 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 100.0% |

Nadir fit: a disk with **R ≤ 11.26 km** has peak \|az\| ≤ sensor
half-FOV. For any such R, **one-axis and two-axis tracking times are the
same** (both are elevation-window limited).

Industrial smoke plumes in the Mommert Sentinel-2 corpus are labelled in
1.2 km tiles; published visible industrial plumes are often a few hundred
metres to a few km. Power-plant SO2 plumes have been traversed at 4–16 km
downwind, so a *long* plume's centroid can sit several km from the stack.
Even a conservative **R = 5–10 km** is still inside the 1-axis nadir swath.

### Superposition of several plumes

If plume *i* has CoG in a disk of radius `r_i` around origin `o_i`, and the
gimbal tracks the weighted centroid `c = Σ w_i p_i` (`w_i ≥ 0`, `Σ w_i = 1`):

- `c` stays in a disk of radius **`R_centroid = Σ w_i r_i`** around `Σ w_i o_i`.
- Equal-radius plumes: `R_centroid = r`, **independent of N**.
- N does **not** add the radii unless the tracker is allowed to put all of
  the weight on one plume (i.e. it switches to a single plume). That case is
  `R = r` around *that* stack, and the stacks' lateral separation is an
  origin offset, not a bigger disk about one origin.
- To keep *every* plume inside the frame, use the covering radius
  `R_cover = max_i (|o_i - o_c| + r_i) = D + r` for origins in a cluster
  of half-span D.

So 'superposition of the radii' is `Σ w_i r_i` for centroid tracking, and
`D + r` for covering a cluster. It is not `N r` unless the origins themselves
are allowed to sit `r` apart in a chain and we must cover all of them.

### Wind during the pass

A CoG that advects at wind speed `v` during the elevation window picks up
an extra ground displacement `v × 108 s`.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.00 | 11.26 |
| 10 | 1.08 | 10.19 |
| 20 | 2.15 | 9.11 |

## If the original analysis was 'anywhere in the ±10 deg box'

That envelope is a **pointing** box, not the sensor FOV. At nadir it is a
**141 km** wide ground strip. The 150 mm sensor only sees
**22.5 km** of that strip. A two-axis gimbal is what lets you
place the 3.2 deg chip anywhere in the 20 deg box.

A stack that sits 40–70 km off the slice centre is inside the two-axis box
and **outside** the one-axis chip for the whole pass — 100% of that
target's tracking time is lost. That is the loss if we still needed to
cover the full ±10 deg operational slice.

| origin cross-track (km) | 1-axis time (s) | 2-axis time (s) | lost (s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 |
| 5 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 |
| 10 | 107.6 | 107.6 | 0.0 |
| 20 | 28.7 | 107.6 | 78.9 |
| 40 | 0.0 | 107.6 | 107.6 |
| 70 | 0.0 | 107.7 | 107.7 |

Under the new model (one origin, CoG in a disk of a few km, slice already
aligned with that origin) that off-axis population is not in the
requirement, and the azimuth axis buys **no extra tracking time**.

## Bottom line (under the defaults above)

1. Along-track, a single elevation axis gives **108 s**
   of track (vs **2.7 s** staring). Peak rate
   1.03 deg/s is under the 2 deg/s cap.
2. Lateral, the 2448 px axis sees ±1.61 deg ≈
   **±11.3 km** at nadir. For R ≲ 11 km the CoG never
   leaves the chip, so dropping azimuth costs **0 s** of tracking time.
3. The azimuth axis only pays for itself if we must (a) keep a CoG on
   boresight rather than merely in-frame, (b) cover origins tens of km
   off the slice centre, or (c) cover a cluster with D + r ≳ 11 km.
4. A two-sided elevation axis (through nadir) would roughly **double**
   along-track time without adding the second motor.
