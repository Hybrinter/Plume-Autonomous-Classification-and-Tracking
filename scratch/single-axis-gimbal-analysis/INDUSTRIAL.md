# Industrial area vs latitude, and expected tracking time

TEMPORARY ANALYSIS. Generated by `industrial_latitude.py`.

## Inventory

Primary: Climate TRACE v5.10.0 CO₂ **point sources** (2025, monthly summed)
that actually have smoke stacks:

- combustion electricity generation (coal / gas / oil / biomass / waste)
- cement, lime, glass
- iron and steel, aluminum, other metals
- chemicals, other chemicals, petrochemical steam cracking
- pulp and paper, other manufacturing
- oil and gas refining

Oil/gas production, mines, hydro/solar/wind, and food plants are out:
they are not the industrial-stack plume the payload is built for.

Nearby sources within 8 km are one plant cluster. Clusters wider than
the chip (~12 km covering radius) are split, matching the earlier rule
that two plants farther apart than the chip are two tracks. Covering
radius is `R = D + 2 km` (plant span + plume CoG).

The size assumption is that industrial-area size scales with the
number of stacks, so the **primary weight is stack count**. Covering-disk
area over-weights sprawling clusters (π D²) and is a sensitivity only.
Emissions weight how much the plant is actually running.

## Expected tracking time (ISS-belt clusters, nadir-centered pass)

For each cluster, `time_lost(|lat|, R)` uses the same one-sided 90→30
deg window, Earth rotation, and hard gimbal stop as `analyze.py`.
A pass is assumed over the cluster CoG (best-case origin). Off-track
origins are a separate loss, captured in the swath column.

| weight | E[T 1-axis] (s) | E[T 2-axis] (s) | E[lost] (s) | lost % | weight at \|lat\|≥45° |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **stack count (primary)** | 50.0 | 121.3 | 71.4 | 58.8% | 10.2% |
| 2025 CO₂ | 42.8 | 121.2 | 78.4 | 64.7% | 6.0% |
| covering-disk area (sprawl) | 22.0 | 121.4 | 99.3 | 81.8% | 8.1% |

ISS dwell (more time near ±i) does not change the story:
stacks + dwell → E[lost] = **58.7 s** (63.0 vs 121.8).

## Lateral swath (why 2-axis still buys something at 50°)

Tracking time is conditional on the cluster being in the frame.
1-axis only sees ±1.60 deg (~12 km at nadir). 2-axis sees the ±10 deg
box (~76 km). Ground-track spacing is thousands of km, so daily
longitude coverage is proportional to swath.

| | 1-axis | 2-axis | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mean daily coverage of a random ISS-belt cluster | 2.26% | 14.22% | 6.31× |
| yield proxy E[T × coverage] (s × frac) | 1.20 | 17.28 | 14.34× |

So even where Earth-rotation azimuth walk is ~0, dropping the azimuth
axis throws away a factor of ~6 in how much industrial area a given
day can put in the frame. That is the lateral-motion cost of one axis,
independent of the along-track 124 s window.

## Decision numbers

1. **Along-track, given a pass over the CoG:** stack-weighted expected
   loss is **71 s of 121 s** (59%).
   Almost all of that is the 20–40° N band (China, US, Med, India,
   Japan/Korea), where Earth rotation walks the covering disk out at
   the 30 deg stop. Only ~10% of stacks sit at \|lat\| ≥ 45°, where
   a 5 km cluster is lossless on one axis.
2. **Lateral, whether we acquire at all:** 2-axis covers ~6× more
   industrial area per day. One axis only works plants that sit under
   the 24 km ground-track ribbon. Combined with shorter tracking time,
   the yield proxy E[T × coverage] is **14.3×** higher with two axes.
3. A polar-slice one-axis payload that is only tasked at \|lat\| ≥ 45°
   keeps the 124 s window, but it is looking at ~10% of the world's
   stack-bearing industry. If the mission must work the 20–40° N
   belt, 2-axis is required for the elevation window, not just swath.
