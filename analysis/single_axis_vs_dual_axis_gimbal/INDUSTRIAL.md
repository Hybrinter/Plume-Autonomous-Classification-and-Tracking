# Industrial area vs latitude, and expected tracking time

TEMPORARY ANALYSIS. Generated by the industry command of
`uv run python -m analysis.studies.single_axis_vs_dual_axis_gimbal`. Not flight software.

## Physical setup

- **Fixed origin.** Elevation tracks the stack, or the cluster centroid of
  stacks. Wind can swing the visible plume around that point during the
  ~120 s window. Tracking plume CoG would add lateral walk this study
  does not model.
- **Covering disk.** Unknown wind azimuth makes a disk of radius **L**
  around each stack. Cluster covering radius is `R = D + L`, with D the
  haversine plant span. That is a possibility set, not a smoke-filled blob.
- **L = 2 km** is a **conservative visible-length envelope**
  (cooling-tower photo climatologies: typical 0.3-0.8 km, winter often
  >0.9 km, ~90-95th percentile ~1.5-3 km). Not a Mommert-chip measurement.
  Not typical length.
- **P(visible)** = fraction of that disk in the FOV (unknown-wind geometry).
  Plume volume / Gaussian ribbon (~5% disk fill) is occupancy/SNR only.
  Do not multiply the two.
- **No locked design R.** Operational R is per cluster (`r_cover_km`) and,
  for lat tables, stack-weighted mean `R(|lat|) = D(|lat|) + L`.
- **Camera/lens.** BFS-U3-50S5, Sony IMX264, 3.45 um, 2448x2048,
  2/3-inch, global shutter; 150 mm athermal, 0.66% distortion. Catalog
  2/3-inch HFOV 3.36 deg is a check. 2x2 mosaic => band plane
  1224x1024, IFOV x2. Ignore lens-catalog 2.74 um.
- **Two slew caps.** Imaging rewind vs ground is the 1-pixel / 1 ms
  **band-plane** gate (current imaging gate). Hardware cap
  **10 deg/s** is not for science frames. SIL
  `ifov=0.02` is not this optic.
- **Reacquire.** After a target leaves the frame both gimbals slew
  to the elevation-window start (hardware cap, not science frames)
  and wait for the next cluster. One-axis search is the FOV ribbon;
  two-axis search is the gimbal box. Lost time is mean reacquire.
  Cycle time is single-target dwell plus reacquire (start of tracking
  until the next target is acquired). Mean encounter is Poisson /
  mean-spacing, ocean-averaged in a lat band, conditional on the ISS
  belt -- not a city-corridor nearest neighbour.

## Inventory

Primary: Climate TRACE v5.10.0 CO2 **point sources** (2025, monthly summed)
that actually have smoke stacks:

- combustion electricity generation (coal / gas / oil / biomass / waste)
- cement, lime, glass
- iron and steel, aluminum, other metals
- chemicals, other chemicals, petrochemical steam cracking
- pulp and paper, other manufacturing
- oil and gas refining

Oil/gas production, mines, hydro/solar/wind, and food plants are out:
they are not the industrial-stack plume the payload is built for.

Nearby sources within 8 km are one plant cluster. Clusters wider than
the chip (~12 km covering radius) are split, matching the earlier rule
that two plants farther apart than the chip are two tracks. Covering
radius is `R = D + 2 km` (plant span + L envelope).

The size assumption is that industrial-area size scales with the
number of stacks, so the **primary weight is stack count**. Covering-disk
area over-weights sprawling clusters (pi D^2) and is a sensitivity only.
Emissions weight how much the plant is actually running.

### Single-target dwell and reacquire at R(|lat|)

T1 / T2 are how long **one** cluster stays in frame, using each
band's stack-weighted mean R. After that target leaves, both
gimbals slew to the 90 deg window start at the hardware cap, then
wait for the next cluster to fly into the search swath at ISS
ground-track speed. 1-axis swath is the FOV ribbon; 2-axis is the
+/-10 deg box. Lost time is T_reacq = t_reset + t_enc. Cycle time
is dwell plus reacquire: start of tracking until the next acquire.
Same-origin leftover T2-T1 is not the lost-time metric.

## Expected times (ISS-belt clusters)

For each cluster, single-target dwell uses that cluster's own
`r_cover_km` and the same one-sided 90->30 deg window as the
geometry command. Reacquire uses lat-binned ocean-averaged cluster
density. A pass is assumed over the cluster origin for dwell;
off-track origins are a separate loss in the swath column.

| weight | dwell 1 (s) | dwell 2 (s) | T_reacq 1 (s) | T_reacq 2 (s) | cycle 1 (s) | cycle 2 (s) | duty 1 | duty 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **stack count (primary)** | 50.0 | 121.3 | 210.6 | 38.4 | 260.5 | 159.8 | 22% | 78% |
| 2025 CO2 | 42.8 | 121.2 | 198.7 | 36.6 | 241.5 | 157.8 | 20% | 78% |
| covering-disk area (sprawl) | 22.0 | 121.4 | 186.8 | 34.7 | 208.8 | 156.1 | 11% | 79% |

ISS dwell (more time near +/-i) does not change single-target dwell:
stacks + dwell -> **63.0 vs 121.8 s** (same-origin leftover 58.7 s).
Lost time is reacquire: **211 s (1-axis) vs 38 s (2-axis)**. Cycle (start of track to next acquire) is **261 s vs 160 s**. Duty T_dwell / cycle is **22% vs 78%**.
Ocean-averaged lat-band density makes encounter times long; city
corridors would reacquire faster. This is not a nearest-neighbour
plant-to-plant model.

## Lateral swath (why 2-axis still buys something at 50 deg)

Tracking time is conditional on the cluster being in the frame.
1-axis only sees +/-1.60 deg (~12 km at nadir). 2-axis sees the +/-10 deg
box (~76 km). Ground-track spacing is thousands of km, so daily
longitude coverage is proportional to swath.

| | 1-axis | 2-axis | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mean daily coverage of a random ISS-belt cluster | 2.26% | 14.22% | 6.31x |
| yield proxy E[T x coverage] (s x frac) | 1.20 | 17.28 | 14.34x |

So even where Earth-rotation azimuth walk is ~0, dropping the azimuth
axis throws away a factor of ~6 in how much industrial area a given
day can put in the frame. That is the lateral-motion cost of one axis,
independent of the along-track 124 s window.

## Decision numbers

1. **Single-target dwell:** stack-weighted **50 s vs 121 s**.
   Almost all of the same-origin gap is the 20-40 N band, where Earth
   rotation walks the covering disk out of the 1-axis chip. That
   number is still the right 'how long can we stare at one plant'
   metric. Only ~10% of stacks sit at |lat| >= 45 deg.
2. **Lost time is reacquire, both gimbals:** after the target leaves,
   slew to the window start and wait for the next cluster. Stack-
   weighted E[T_reacq] is **211 s (1-axis ribbon) vs 38 s (2-axis box)**.
   Cycle start-of-track to next-acquire is
   **261 s vs 160 s** (duty 22% vs 78%).
3. **Lateral, whether we acquire at all:** 2-axis covers ~6x more
   industrial area per day. One axis only works plants that sit under
   the 24 km ground-track ribbon. Combined with shorter dwell,
   the yield proxy E[T_dwell x coverage] is **14.3x** higher with two axes.
4. A polar-slice one-axis payload that is only tasked at |lat| >= 45 deg
   keeps the 124 s dwell, but it is looking at ~10% of the world's
   stack-bearing industry. If the mission must work the 20-40 N
   belt, 2-axis is required for dwell *and* for faster reacquire.
